Car Dealership Management System - Database Requirements

1. Purpose
The database supports the day-to-day operations of a car dealership. It stores consistent, auditable information about:
  - the vehicle inventory (manufacturers, models and individual cars)
  - customers and employees
  - car sales and the payments made towards them
  - the service workshop (service records, assigned staff and parts used)

Data integrity is enforced at database level through primary and foreign keys, unique and check constraints, stored procedures and triggers. The same model is implemented on two platforms: Oracle (PL/SQL) and Microsoft SQL Server (T-SQL).

2. Scope
In scope:
  - inventory management: cars grouped by manufacturer and model, each with a VIN, price, mileage and status
  - customer and employee records, including employment periods
  - sales lifecycle tracking through sale statuses
  - payment recording, including multiple partial payments per sale
  - service and maintenance history: service type, labor cost, assigned employees and parts used
  - reporting: monthly sales and service revenue

Out of scope:
  - user accounts, roles and access control
  - car financing contracts, insurance and warranties
  - trade-ins, discounts and loyalty programs
  - parts stock levels and supplier ordering

3. Entities
  - Manufacturer: car brand and its country of origin
  - CarModel: model name and body type, belonging to one manufacturer
  - Car: a single physical vehicle in the inventory
  - Customer: a person who buys a car or brings a car in for service
  - Employee: a member of staff who handles sales and service work
  - Sale: the sale of one car to one customer, handled by one employee
  - SaleStatus: lookup of sale states (PENDING, ON_HOLD, COMPLETED, CANCELLED, REFUNDED)
  - Payment: a payment made towards a sale
  - PaymentMethod: lookup of payment methods (CASH, CREDIT_CARD, DEBIT_CARD, BANK_TRANSFER, FINANCING)
  - ServiceRecord: a single service visit for a car
  - ServiceType: lookup of service types (e.g. OIL_CHANGE, BRAKE_SERVICE, GENERAL_INSPECTION)
  - ServiceEmployee: assignment of an employee to a service record, with their role
  - Part: a spare part with its part number and current price
  - PartsUsed: a part used during a service, with quantity and the price charged at that time

4. Relationships
  - A manufacturer produces one or more car models; each car model belongs to exactly one manufacturer.
  - Each car is of exactly one car model; a car model can have many cars.
  - A sale links exactly one car, one customer and one employee, and has exactly one sale status.
  - A customer and an employee can each be part of many sales; a car can appear in several sales over time (e.g. after a cancellation).
  - A sale can have zero or more payments; each payment uses exactly one payment method.
  - A service record belongs to exactly one car and one customer and has exactly one service type.
  - A service record can have many employees assigned (many-to-many via ServiceEmployee).
  - A service record can use many parts, and a part can be used in many services (many-to-many via PartsUsed).

5. Data Integrity Requirements
5.1 Keys and Referential Integrity
  - Every table has a primary key; identifiers are generated automatically (IDENTITY).
  - All foreign keys must reference existing parent rows.
  - Master data referenced by sales, payments or service records cannot be deleted, so that history is preserved.

5.2 Uniqueness
  - Car VIN, customer email, employee email and part number are unique.
  - A model name is unique within its manufacturer.
  - Lookup names (sale status, payment method, service type) are unique.
  - The same part appears at most once per service record (quantity is stored instead).
  - The same employee is assigned at most once per service record.

5.3 Domain Constraints
  - Car status is one of: AVAILABLE, RESERVED, SOLD, IN_SERVICE.
  - Car year is between 1886 and 2100; mileage, when known, is >= 0.
  - Car list price, sale price and payment amount must be > 0.
  - Labor cost, part price and unit price at service must be >= 0; part quantity must be > 0.
  - An employee's end of employment date, when set, cannot be earlier than the start date.

6. Business Rules
6.1 Sale Rules
  - A new sale must start with the status PENDING or ON_HOLD.
  - A sale cannot be created for a car that is SOLD or IN_SERVICE.
  - The car of a COMPLETED sale cannot be changed.
  - A sale can be set to COMPLETED only when the total paid equals the sale price.

6.2 Car Status Synchronization
  - While a sale is PENDING or ON_HOLD, the car is RESERVED.
  - When a sale becomes COMPLETED, the car becomes SOLD.
  - When a sale is CANCELLED or REFUNDED, the car returns to AVAILABLE, unless it has another open sale.
  Implementation: row-level sale trigger (Oracle: trg_sale_biud, SQL Server: trg_sale_aiu).

6.3 Payment Rules
  - Each payment must reference an existing sale and payment method.
  - Partial payments are allowed; a sale can have several payments.
  - The total of all payments for a sale must never exceed the sale price.
  - When the total paid reaches the sale price, the sale is automatically marked COMPLETED.
  Implementation: payment trigger (Oracle: compound trigger trg_payment_compound, SQL Server: trg_payment_aiu).

6.4 Sale Finalization
  - A sale can be finalized on request; the database checks that it is fully paid, then sets the sale to COMPLETED and the car to SOLD.
  - Finalizing an already completed sale has no effect and returns an informative message.
  Implementation: stored procedure (Oracle: sp_finalize_sale, SQL Server: usp_FinalizeSale).

6.5 Service Rules
  - Each service record must reference an existing car, customer and service type.
  - The cost of a service is its labor cost plus the sum of (quantity x unit price at service) for all parts used.
  - The price of a part is stored at the time of service, so later price changes do not alter past service costs.

7. Reporting Requirements
The database must support the following queries:
  1. Available cars with manufacturer and model details (inventory listing).
  2. Sales history with customer, employee, car and sale status.
  3. Total payments per sale and the remaining balance (paid vs. sale price).
  4. Top customers by total spending (completed sales and payments).
  5. Monthly KPIs: completed sales revenue and service revenue (labor + parts).
     Implementation: cursor-based stored procedure (Oracle: sp_monthly_kpis, SQL Server: usp_MonthlyKpis).
  6. Service records with service type, assigned employees and a breakdown of parts used.
  7. Cars that currently cannot be sold (IN_SERVICE or SOLD) and the reason.
  8. Service workload per employee (number of service records each employee took part in).

8. Assumptions
  - A car is sold to one customer at a time; earlier cancelled or refunded sales remain stored for history.
  - Sales, payments and service records are never deleted; they are kept for auditing and reporting.
  - Lookup values (sale statuses, payment methods, service types) are loaded with the seed data.
  - All monetary values are stored in a single currency.
